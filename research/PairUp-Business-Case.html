Executive summary

Small local trades are worth billions, and most of them never happen

The opportunity

PairUp lets anyone say what they need, have or can do. It pairs the two sides, settles the price inside limits each side set privately, proposes a public hand-off point and asks both people for a yes. Things, errands, hourly help and event staffing all run through the same engine, by voice.

Why it wins

  • Cheaper for both sides. The agreed price passes in full from buyer to seller. On a $20 calculator, the seller receives $18.00 on PairUp against $16.00–17.12 on resale sites, and the buyer pays $18.00 against $20 plus shipping.
  • Faster. One sentence replaces photos, a listing form, chat and haggling. Requests stand, so the next matching seller is paired the moment they post.
  • Built for agents. Any AI assistant connects over the open MCP standard. Prices come from a published, open-source rule, so agents never haggle and cannot be talked into a worse deal. Research shows AI negotiators can be swayed by tone.15
  • Strong fit with Amazon. Alexa+ is the voice, Amazon Hub Lockers and Counters are the hand-off points, amazon.com covers every need no neighbor can meet, and AWS runs it.

Recommendation. Launch at the University of Washington in Seattle: 52,316 students in walking distance with needs that repeat every quarter. Our model puts one campus at about $1.9 million a year of trades between students. Measure for one quarter, then expand campus by campus.

The problem

Trading with the person next door is slow, costly and risky

Five pains, each backed by public data.

PainEvidence
Fees eat small salesPoshmark keeps 20% of sales of $15 and up; Mercari 10% plus 2.9% + $0.30 for payment; eBay about 12.9–15% plus $0.30 per order.2
Small errands cost more than the errandA $39 restaurant meal cost $47.10 on the major delivery apps before service fees.5 Grocery items averaged 11% above in-store prices, plus delivery fees.6 Hired help adds a service fee and a Trust & Support fee on each hour.7
Strangers are riskyAmericans reported $12.5 billion lost to fraud in 2024, up 25%. Online shopping was the second most reported category, with $432 million lost.8
Haggling leaks and stallsOn millions of eBay Best Offer negotiations, the listing price itself signalled how eager a seller was: round-number listings drew offers 8–12% lower.13 Bargaining with private values loses deals both sides wanted.12
Useful things are thrown awayThe US generated 12.1 million tons of furniture waste in 2018; 80.1% went to landfill.9 Students are budgeted about $1,290 a year for books and supplies.10

Why the market skips these trades

Economics has explained this for decades. Searching costs time, so buyers settle for worse prices (Stigler, 1961).11 Every market trade carries costs of finding, negotiating and enforcing it, and trades worth less than those costs never happen (Coase, 1937).11 A $4 lunch run and a $15 lamp sit squarely below that line today.

What this means. The demand and the supply already exist on every campus and street. The missing piece is a way to connect them that costs one sentence and charges no commission on the deal.

Trust built in

Private limits never leave the server. A price check asks "did you mean…?" on prices far from normal and warns buyers about deals that look too good. Banned items and services are refused with a reason. Ratings build each person's track record, reports pause pairing until reviewed, and hand-offs happen at public Amazon Hub points.

Why campuses first

Campuses are dense and walkable. Needs repeat every quarter (textbooks, calculators), and move-in and move-out refill supply every year. Student clubs bring organization demand. Each new campus is a self-contained market that reaches density quickly.

Why now · C2C in the future

Personal agents will trade for people. They need a fair place to meet.

Commerce is moving from apps people browse to assistants that act for them. Research on AI agents in markets points to three requirements, and PairUp meets each one.

What research findsSourcePairUp's answer
AI negotiators show anchoring; one acting desperate gains about 20%Bianchi et al., ICML 202415Agents never bargain. One published rule in open-source pairing-core sets every price.
AI shoppers favor the first offer they receive, and quality falls as markets growMagentic Marketplace, Microsoft Research 202516The server ranks every fitting offer before an agent sees it, so the best match wins.
Markets of AI agents need new institutions to work wellHadfield & Koh, NBER Handbook 202524An open protocol (MCP), shared test vectors for the price rule, and a required human yes.
No bilateral trading rule is perfect on every countMyerson & Satterthwaite 198312A simple rule with its trade-offs published: public ask, automatic best-offer acceptance.

Open by design

  • Any assistant connects: Alexa+, a Strands agent on Bedrock AgentCore, or any MCP client, through OAuth 2.1 with Login with Amazon.
  • Any host settles the same way: pairing-core is MIT-licensed Rust compiled to WebAssembly, with shared test vectors.
  • People stay in charge: agents find, explain and prepare; people confirm. Every person can be a buyer and a seller at once, through the assistant they already use.

Risks and how we handle them

RiskPlan
Too few people at firstStart on one dense campus; requests stand until a match arrives; move-in, move-out and club events bring both sides at once.
Safety between strangersSigned-in accounts, ratings, reports that pause pairing, banned-item rules, a too-good-to-be-true warning, and public Amazon Hub meet-ups.
Agents gaming pricesPrices come from code; one offer below the ask per buyer and listing; limits never leave the server; a human yes on every deal.
Mistyped or misheard pricesThe price check asks "did you mean…?" against reference prices and the market's own trades, weighted so staged trades barely move it.
PaymentPeople pay each other at the hand-off today; in-app payment through Amazon is the natural next step.

Recommendation

  1. Launch the UW Seattle pilot with students on both sides: requesters and providers.
  2. Measure for one quarter: time to deal, price against alternatives, seller earnings, unaided success.
  3. Connect Amazon services: the Hub locator for hand-offs and live amazon.com prices for the fallback.
  4. Expand to the next Seattle campus once the first reaches steady weekly trades.

# UW campus pilot

Status: planned. Results go in the table below once sessions run.

## Question

Can a UW student find or offer something useful, understand the proposed cost and time, and agree or decline without help? Does speaking save effort compared with how they arrange this today (group chats, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist)?

Recruit a small group from the UW community, with both requesters and providers. Tell each person the posts are test posts, no money changes hands and voice is optional. Use aliases and ask before recording. Keep budgets, addresses and raw voice recordings out of the public report.

## Session (about ten minutes per person)

1. Ask how they last found a cheap item or a quick campus errand. Note the method and the friction in their words.
2. Give a goal, not a script: find a calculator for an exam within a stated budget. Watch sign-in, the first request, reading the card and the yes or no. Let them work it out before helping.
3. Switch roles: offer two hours of help on a given day. Ask whether a proposed job outside those hours should be accepted. Check that they understood the schedule and the total.
4. Ask them to find a request again after signing out and back in, and to withdraw a post.
5. Ask: “What did you just agree to?”, “What will you pay in total?”, “Who still needs to approve?”, “Would you use this for a real need? Why?”

For a real exchange later, record agreement and the actual hand-off or completed job as separate outcomes.

## Report

State how people were recruited, the dates, sample size, device and browser, mock or Bedrock mode, and that the posts were test data. Report unaided success and correct understanding of cost and time as counts out of the total, including failed tasks. Give the median time with its range. Fix the most common confusion, then rerun that task with new participants.
